Theory side: a lightly-damped bob attracted by three magnets on the triangular board, hanging from the hub on a string. Chaos shows up as tiny start-position changes flipping the final magnet — compute the basin map to see the fractal boundary.

Real drops on our magnetic board

We release the pendulum bob from different spots on the triangular board. It swings, and one of the three magnets catches it. Every dot on the map is one real drop: where the dot is = where we let the bob go, the dot's color = which magnet won.

How the software measures a drop
1
A phone camera films the board from above while we release the bob.
2
The software finds the three magnets and the center hub in the video automatically — no manual marking.
3
It follows the white bob in every single frame and measures exactly where it stops.
4
The nearest magnet to the stopping point wins, and the drop is added to the map above.
When a recording fails

Sometimes a recording can't be measured (the bob hides a magnet, or the camera shakes). Then the computer simulates what would have happened using the physics model — dashed golden arrows on the map. These predictions are clearly marked and never mixed with real measurements.
